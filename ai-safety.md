      <h1 class="as-h1" data-copy="AS-HERO-H1">From children's privacy to <em>youth AI safety</em>.</h1>

      <p class="as-lede" data-copy="AS-HERO-P1">
        I study how to protect young people's privacy and safety in technologies they are required to use and
        those they choose for themselves. My research began with schools, where institutions make technology
        decisions on students' behalf, and now examines how these responsibilities change as young people use AI
        for schoolwork and personal support.
      </p>

      <p class="as-lede" data-copy="AS-HERO-P2">
        I combine <strong>human-computer interaction</strong>, <strong>computational methods</strong>, and
        <strong>legal analysis</strong> to guide the design and governance of these systems.
      </p>

    <div class="as-inner">
      <div class="as-label" data-copy="AS-PILLARS-LABEL">How privacy led to AI safety</div>
      <p class="as-sublabel" data-copy="AS-PILLARS-SUBLABEL">The work moves from technology schools choose for students to technology students choose for themselves. In both cases I ask what is collected about young people and who is responsible for it.</p>

      <div class="as-pillars">

        <div class="as-pillar">
          <div class="as-pillar-kicker" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-1-KICKER">Required &middot; Schools decide</div>
          <h2 class="as-pillar-title" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-1-TITLE">Who controls young people's information</h2>
          <p data-copy="AS-PILLAR-1-BODY">
            Students generally cannot opt out of the software their school adopts. I study what that software
            collects in K&ndash;12 settings, how vendors behave against their stated practices, and who is
            responsible for securing it. I have found that collection is extensive while oversight is limited, and
            that schools are rarely able to account for what student data leaves their systems.
          </p>
          <div class="as-chips">

          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="as-pillar">
          <div class="as-pillar-kicker" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-2-KICKER">Required &middot; Schools set AI rules</div>
          <h2 class="as-pillar-title" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-2-TITLE">How institutions govern AI use</h2>
          <p data-copy="AS-PILLAR-2-BODY">
            Many students first encounter generative AI through school rules: a district acceptable-use policy, a
            vendor contract, or a detector pointed at their homework. I use privacy frameworks to examine how those
            rules get written, what information flows they permit, and who bears the cost when a rule's
            assumptions are wrong.
          </p>
          <div class="as-chips">
            <span class="cv-badge badge-gold">FAccT '26</span>

            <a href="/research" class="as-pillar-link" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-2-LINK">Full thread &rarr;</a>

        </div>

        <div class="as-pillar">
          <div class="as-pillar-kicker" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-3-KICKER">Chosen &middot; Students decide</div>
          <h2 class="as-pillar-title" data-copy="AS-PILLAR-3-TITLE">When young people want AI assistance</h2>
          <p data-copy="AS-PILLAR-3-BODY">
            Many young people now use AI on their own, for schoolwork and for personal support, often without any
            institution reviewing the tool. My FAccT 2026 study found that high school students draw careful lines
            between legitimate AI help and cheating. Two papers under review extend this work. One examines what
            students expect to happen to information they share with AI for schoolwork; the other examines when
            teens trust an AI agent's online safety advice over advice from people in their lives.
          </p>

            <p data-copy="AS-CLAIM-2-BODY">

        <p data-copy="AS-WORKSHOP-BODY">

          organize a workshop series at ACM CHI and ASSETS that brings researchers, designers, educators, and
          policymakers together to build one. My own contribution focuses on privacy and governance, particularly
          what happens to young people's information when they use AI systems.
